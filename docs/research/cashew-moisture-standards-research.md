# Cashew Moisture Standards — Research Notes

## Sprint
Sprint 11

## Article
**Working title:** Cashew Moisture Standards: Measurement, Quality, and Storage

## Research objective
Establish a technically reliable factual foundation for a Mato Cashew Knowledge Center article about moisture content in cashew kernels. The article must distinguish product stages, standards/specifications, analytical methods, and practical interpretation without presenting one market specification as a universal global rule.

## Core research findings

### 1. AFI moisture specification
The Association of Food Industries (AFI) Specifications for Cashew Kernels, adopted by the Nut & Agricultural Product Section in September 2023, state that the moisture level of cashews shall be **3%–5%**.

AFI specifies that moisture is determined by a **Loss on Drying** method using:
- a moisture balance at **105°C**;
- **AACCI Method 44-15 (modified)**; or
- an equivalent loss-on-drying method that correlates with **AOAC Official Method 925.40, Moisture in Nuts and Nut Products**, or an equivalent vacuum-oven method.

**Interpretation rule:** Present this as an **AFI cashew-kernel specification**, not as a universal global moisture requirement.

### 2. UNECE DDP-17
UNECE maintains **DDP-17 — Cashew Kernels**, with the current standards listing showing the **2023 edition**. The standard applies to cashew kernels intended for commercial and marketing quality control.

Our reviewed 2023 DDP-17 text uses a **maximum moisture content of 5.0%** for cashew kernels.

**Interpretation rule:** Present UNECE and AFI separately because their wording and scope are not identical.

### 3. AFI and UNECE should not be merged into one “global standard”
The two references broadly converge around a 5% upper level, but:
- AFI expresses a **3%–5% range**.
- UNECE DDP-17 expresses a **maximum of 5.0%**.
- They are issued by different bodies and serve different commercial contexts.

Recommended wording:
> “AFI specifies 3%–5% moisture for cashew kernels, while UNECE DDP-17 sets a maximum moisture content of 5.0%. These should be cited according to their own scope rather than combined into a single universal standard.”

## Product-stage distinction

### Raw cashew nut vs finished cashew kernel
Moisture values must always be interpreted in relation to the product stage.

Suggested learning model:

Raw cashew nut  
→ conditioning / steaming  
→ shelling  
→ kernel drying  
→ peeling / grading  
→ finished cashew kernel  
→ storage / shipment

Research on pre-shelling cashew nuts studies moisture at levels that can be substantially higher than finished-kernel commercial specifications. Those values should **not** be directly compared with the AFI or UNECE finished-kernel limits.

### Why the distinction matters
Moisture can have different functions at different stages:
- During pre-shelling/processing, moisture affects shelling behavior and kernel recovery.
- During kernel drying, moisture is reduced toward a finished-product target.
- In finished kernels, moisture becomes a commercial quality and storage-stability parameter.

## Why moisture matters

### Quality
Moisture is one of the measurable properties used in cashew quality control. Improper moisture can influence handling characteristics and product stability.

### Processing
Peer-reviewed cashew processing studies show that moisture interacts with steaming/drying conditions and can affect whole-kernel recovery and physical behavior.

### Storage
Finished-kernel moisture is closely related to storage protection. AFI requires packaging to be new, clean, dry, leakproof and hermetically sealed, reinforcing the connection between product moisture and moisture control during storage.

### Food safety
The article may explain that moisture control is relevant to mold and storage hygiene, but it should **link to Cashew Food Safety** rather than duplicate hazard-control content.

## Measurement principle

### Loss on Drying
For the target audience, explain the principle rather than writing a laboratory SOP:

1. Obtain a representative sample.
2. Record the initial sample mass.
3. Dry the sample under a specified controlled method.
4. Record the final mass.
5. Interpret the loss in mass as moisture according to the required method/calculation.

### Method-control rule
Commercial acceptance testing should follow the method required by the relevant specification, buyer, laboratory procedure, or contract. Do not imply that a household moisture meter is automatically equivalent to an official analytical method.

## Wet basis vs dry basis

### Wet basis
Moisture is expressed relative to the total mass of the wet sample.

### Dry basis
Moisture is expressed relative to the mass of dry solids.

### Editorial rule
Do not compare numerical values from scientific papers, specifications, or processing studies unless their moisture basis and product stage are clear.

The article should explain the distinction briefly; it does not need to become a calculation tutorial.

## High and low moisture — factual boundary
Avoid universal statements such as:
- “Above X% always causes mold.”
- “Below X% always causes breakage.”

Supported editorial framing:
- Excess moisture can reduce storage stability and contribute to quality/safety concerns.
- Moisture affects physical behavior during processing and handling.
- Final acceptance should be interpreted against the applicable specification or buyer requirement.

## Moisture content vs water activity
Moisture content and water activity are **not the same measurement**.

For Sprint 11:
- A brief distinction may be included.
- Do **not** publish numerical water-activity thresholds unless cashew-specific authoritative evidence is separately validated.
- Water activity should not become a major section in this article.
