# Cashew Export Guide — Research Notes

## Sprint
Sprint 14

## Working title
**Cashew Export Guide: From Product Readiness to International Shipment**

## Research objective
Establish a reliable framework for explaining cashew export readiness without presenting one universal legal checklist for every shipment or destination.

## Core principle
**Export readiness is a layered process, not one universal document list.**

Product definition  
→ Buyer / contract requirements  
→ Quality & food safety  
→ Packaging / marking  
→ Export-side documentation  
→ Origin / phytosanitary checks  
→ Destination-market requirements  
→ Logistics / customs  
→ Traceable shipment

---

## 1. UNECE DDP-17 scope

UNECE DDP-17 is a commercial / marketing quality standard for cashew kernels.

It can support discussion of:
- product definition;
- commercial quality;
- sizing / tolerances;
- presentation / packaging;
- marking.

**Safeguard:** UNECE DDP-17 is not a complete export law, customs procedure, or destination-country food-import regulation.

Official source:
https://unece.org/sites/default/files/2023-10/ECE_CTCS_WP.7_2023_19_E.pdf

---

## 2. Cambodia exporter-side framework

Cambodia’s customs process uses export declarations for goods leaving the customs territory.

High-level export-side model:

Cambodian exporter  
→ prepare shipment / transaction data  
→ lodge customs export declaration  
→ provide applicable supporting documents  
→ customs processing / clearance  
→ shipment

GDCE references export declaration models including EX1 for exportation.

**Scope boundary:** The Knowledge article should explain the process at a high level and should not become a field-by-field SAD/customs-declaration tutorial.

Official sources:
- https://customs.gov.kh/en/customs-procedures
- https://tools.customs.gov.kh/documents/reader/7391/16572508337948228/ministerial-decree-1447

---

## 3. Certificate of Origin — Cambodia

Cambodia’s Ministry of Commerce administers Certificate of Origin systems.

C/O categories include:
- preferential origin;
- non-preferential origin.

Preferential origin depends on:
- applicable trade agreement;
- rules of origin;
- documentary evidence;
- transaction context.

**Safeguard:** Do not state that one identical Certificate of Origin is mandatory for every cashew shipment.

Official sources:
- https://co.moc.gov.kh/About
- https://ftaportal.moc.gov.kh/

---

## 4. Phytosanitary requirements

Cambodia’s plant-health authority provides phytosanitary inspection and certification procedures for consignments subject to phytosanitary requirements.

The actual need for phytosanitary certification depends on:
- commodity form;
- processing status;
- origin;
- destination-country plant-health requirements;
- current inspection/certification rules.

**Critical distinction:** Raw cashew nuts (RCN) and processed cashew kernels must not be assumed to have identical plant-health requirements.

Official source:
https://www.maff.gov.kh/gda/departmentdocs/387LgUr3R6?lang=en

---

## 5. U.S. example — plant-health layer

USDA APHIS guidance indicates that certain dried, cured, cooked, or processed plant products, including nuts, may be generally admissible without an APHIS import permit or phytosanitary certificate, subject to applicable conditions and exceptions.

**Safeguard:** Do not generalize this to every cashew commodity form, origin, or shipment.

Important conceptual distinction:

Processed cashew kernels  
≠  
Raw / less-processed plant commodity

Actual plant-health treatment depends on commodity + origin + destination + current rule.

Official sources:
- https://www.aphis.usda.gov/ace/generally-authorized-non-propagative-plant-products
- https://www.aphis.usda.gov/plant-imports/commodity-approval

---

## 6. U.S. example — food-import layer

FDA food-import requirements are separate from USDA APHIS plant-health rules.

Potential U.S. food-import obligations may include, where applicable:
- food-facility registration;
- Prior Notice;
- importer-side FSVP responsibilities;
- compliance with U.S. food requirements.

**Critical distinction:**
FDA food-import requirements ≠ USDA APHIS plant-health requirements.

Official sources:
- https://www.fda.gov/food/food-imports-exports/importing-food-products-united-states
- https://www.fda.gov/food/guidance-regulation-food-and-dietary-supplements/registration-food-facilities-and-other-submissions
- https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/guidance-industry-what-you-need-know-about-prior-notice-imported-food-shipments

## 7. EU example

EU import controls depend on the product, origin, regulatory area, and current risk controls.

Food of non-animal origin may be subject to:
- general food requirements;
- contaminant limits;
- product/origin-specific increased controls where applicable;
- other applicable official controls.

**Safeguard:** Do not present one fixed EU import checklist for all cashew shipments.

Official sources:
- https://food.ec.europa.eu/horizontal-topics/official-controls-and-enforcement/imported-products_en
- https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/chemical-safety/contaminants/catalogue/aflatoxins_en

## 8. Export document taxonomy

Use document categories rather than a universal mandatory list.

### Commercial
Examples:
- commercial invoice / transaction data;
- buyer / seller contract information.

### Packing / lot
Examples:
- packing information;
- lot / batch identification;
- traceability data.

### Customs
Examples:
- export customs declaration;
- applicable customs supporting documents.

### Origin
Examples:
- Certificate of Origin where applicable;
- preferential / non-preferential origin evidence.

### Plant health
Examples:
- phytosanitary certificate where required.

### Transport
Examples:
- bill of lading;
- air waybill;
- other mode-specific transport documents.

### Testing / inspection / certification
Examples:
- laboratory certificate;
- inspection certificate;
- health or other competent-authority documentation where required.

**Safeguard:** Applicability depends on transaction, product, buyer, destination, transport mode, and current regulation.

---

## 9. Responsibility model

### Exporter / seller
Typical responsibilities may include:
- product definition;
- buyer specification;
- packaging / marking;
- export-side documentation;
- traceability;
- export customs process.

### Buyer / importer
Typical responsibilities may include:
- destination-country import obligations;
- importer-side filings / notifications;
- buyer verification;
- destination compliance.

### Authorities / service providers
May include:
- customs;
- plant-health authority;
- Certificate of Origin issuer;
- laboratory / inspection body;
- freight forwarder;
- customs broker.

**Safeguard:** Do not imply the exporter personally performs every import-side filing.

---

## 10. Labeling / marking boundary

Export Guide may explain high-level marking and traceability principles:
- product identity;
- lot / batch;
- grade where applicable;
- weight;
- origin;
- allergen / consumer information where applicable;
- buyer / market-specific markings.

**Boundary:** Do not create a country-by-country labeling-law manual.

Always advise verification of current destination-market labeling rules before shipment.

---

## 11. Research safeguards

- [ ] No universal export-document checklist.
- [ ] UNECE DDP-17 is not described as complete export law.
- [ ] RCN ≠ processed-kernel export requirements.
- [ ] C/O is not described as universally mandatory in one fixed form.
- [ ] Phytosanitary certificate is not described as universally mandatory.
- [ ] U.S. FDA ≠ USDA APHIS.
- [ ] Exporter responsibilities ≠ importer responsibilities.
- [ ] Buyer requirements may exceed regulatory minimums.
- [ ] EU requirements remain product/origin/current-rule specific.
- [ ] Current destination requirements must be re-verified close to shipment time.
- [ ] Static article does not replace customs broker, importer, regulator, or competent authority.
- [ ] No tariff table or permanent legal-compliance guarantee.
