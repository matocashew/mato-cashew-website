## Objective
Establish the factual foundation for Mato Cashew's article on packaging finished cashew kernels.

## Core rule
**Packaging requirement ≠ packaging technology ≠ universal best practice.**

## AFI — Specifications for Cashew Kernels (September 2023)
AFI applies its specification to shipments to the U.S. It requires new, clean, dry, leakproof, lead-free containers with an airtight (hermetic) seal and sufficient strength to protect product integrity during normal shipment and storage. The outer container must be free of infestation and visible mold.

AFI also states that only food-grade CO2 is permitted, at a minimum mixture of 60% CO2, with the balance nitrogen.

**Safeguard:** This gas provision is AFI/U.S.-shipment context. Do not present it as a universal requirement for all cashew markets.

AFI labeling/traceability provisions include product identification, producer/packer, grade, weight, origin, allergen marking, lot/production/date codes, and buyer/seller-agreed marks. Packing lists support lot traceability.

Source: https://afius.org/afi-specifications-for-cashew-kernels/

## UNECE DDP-17 — Cashew Kernels (2023)
UNECE requires cashew kernels to be packed so as to protect the produce properly. Materials inside the package must be clean and of a quality that avoids external or internal damage. Cashew kernels must be packed in hermetically sealed containers. Printing/labelling materials must use non-toxic ink or glue.

**Safeguard:** Cite UNECE separately from AFI. Their scopes and detailed wording are not identical.

Sources:
- https://unece.org/trade/wp7/DDP-Standards
- https://unece.org/sites/default/files/2024-02/DDP17_CashewKernels_2023_e.pdf

## What packaging must achieve
A packaging system may contribute to:
1. Moisture protection.
2. Atmosphere/oxygen management.
3. Contamination protection.
4. Physical protection.
5. Seal integrity.
6. Storage and shipment integrity.
7. Identification and traceability.

Performance depends on product, format, buyer, market, shelf-life objective, distribution environment, and applicable food-contact/regulatory requirements.

## Packaging-system layers
### Primary packaging
Direct product protection: food-contact suitability, barrier performance, sealing, atmosphere control, contamination protection.

### Secondary packaging
Groups/protects primary packages and may provide added handling/mechanical protection.

### Transport packaging
Supports stacking, handling, transport, and distribution integrity.

**Safeguard:** Not every supply chain must use three physically separate layers.

## Hermetic packaging
Both current primary cashew references support hermetic protection. Hermetic packaging limits uncontrolled exchange between the package environment and the external environment.

**Hermetic does not automatically mean vacuum.** Seal integrity is central.

## Vacuum packaging
Vacuum packaging reduces air in a package before sealing. Cashew technical literature documents vacuum/hermetic approaches.

Do not claim vacuum is universally best. Suitability depends on product condition, material, sealing process, physical effects, logistics, shelf-life objective, and buyer/specification requirements.

AFI defines “blocking” as bonding of kernels in the presence of high moisture and high vacuum pressure, showing that package conditions can interact with product condition.

## Gas-flushed / modified-atmosphere packaging
Gas-flushed or modified-atmosphere packaging changes the internal package atmosphere and may manage oxygen exposure.

General nut research can explain mechanisms, but findings from other nuts must not be rewritten as cashew-specific requirements.

Do not claim that cashews must always be nitrogen flushed, that 100% nitrogen is a global cashew standard, or that the AFI gas mixture applies to every market.

## Packaging materials and barrier properties
Explain performance rather than prescribe one laminate. Relevant properties include moisture barrier, oxygen barrier, sealability, puncture/mechanical strength, packing-process compatibility, and food-contact suitability.

Potential formats include flexible barrier systems and rigid containers.

**Safeguard:** No universal film, laminate, plastic grade, or rigid-container material is approved.

## Moisture / storage relationship
Knowledge relationship: **Cashew Moisture Standards → Packaging Guide → Cashew Storage Guide**.

Packaging protects finished product condition. Do not repeat moisture measurement or full warehouse-storage guidance.

## Oxygen / oxidative quality
Packaging can influence oxygen exposure and forms part of an overall quality-preservation system. Do not promise shelf life solely because a package is vacuumed, gas flushed, or high barrier.

## Hygiene and food safety
Cover clean/suitable packaging, contamination prevention, package integrity, food-contact suitability, and relevant non-toxic printing/labelling principles. Do not duplicate the full Food Safety article.

## Bulk vs retail
Bulk/commercial priorities may include protection, barrier/seal performance, shipment integrity, lot identification, traceability, and buyer/specification compliance.

Retail packaging may additionally consider consumer presentation, portioning, convenience, resealability, and retail labeling.

Do not provide country-by-country retail labeling law.

## Shelf-life boundary
Packaging is one factor in shelf-life performance. Product condition, moisture, oxygen exposure, seal integrity, storage conditions, handling, and time also matter. Detailed validation belongs in a future Shelf Life Guide.
