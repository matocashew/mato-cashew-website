# Cashew Customer Complaint & Corrective Action Register: A Practical Business Toolkit

## Introduction

Customer complaints are an important source of information for cashew businesses. Even when products are inspected before delivery, customers may report problems involving product quality, grading, packaging, weight, labeling, or delivery.

For example, a buyer may receive cashew kernels with more broken pieces than agreed, damaged packaging, an incorrect product grade, or a quantity that does not match the delivery documents.

If these issues are not recorded and followed up properly, businesses may find it difficult to identify repeated problems, understand their causes, and demonstrate how they were resolved.

The **Mato Cashew Customer Complaint & Corrective Action Register** is a practical recordkeeping toolkit designed to help cashew processors, traders, wholesalers, exporters, and agricultural cooperatives manage customer complaints in an organized and consistent way.

## 1. Purpose of the Toolkit

The purpose of this toolkit is to provide a simple system for recording complaints, investigating problems, assigning corrective actions, and monitoring their completion.

The register can help businesses:

* Record customer complaints in one place.
* Identify affected products and production batches.
* Review possible causes using available evidence.
* Assign responsibilities and completion deadlines.
* Track corrective actions and customer follow-up.
* Identify recurring complaints that may require process improvements.

The toolkit is intended for day-to-day business operations and can be adapted to different business sizes.

## 3. Common Customer Complaints in Cashew Businesses

Customer complaints may involve different aspects of product quality and service.

**Product quality:** Incorrect grades, excessive broken kernels, unexpected appearance, or differences from agreed specifications.

**Packaging:** Damaged bags, broken seals, incorrect packaging materials, or unclear labeling.

**Quantity and weight:** Differences between delivered quantities and agreed orders.

**Delivery:** Incorrect products, missing items, or delivery discrepancies.

**Product condition:** Unusual odor, moisture concerns, visible deterioration, or suspected contamination.

Complaints suggesting a potential food-safety hazard should be escalated immediately under the business's food-safety procedures. The affected product may need to be identified, isolated, or otherwise controlled while the risk is assessed.

## 4. Recommended Complaint Handling Process

### Step 1 — Receive and Register the Complaint

Record the complaint as soon as it is received. Assign a complaint ID and document the customer, product, batch number, date, and reported issue.

Supporting documents may include photographs, purchase orders, invoices, delivery notes, or customer messages.

### Step 2 — Assess the Complaint

Determine the type and seriousness of the issue.

Consider whether the complaint involves a commercial discrepancy, a product-quality problem, or a possible food-safety concern.

If there is a potential food-safety risk, follow the business's escalation and product-control procedures without waiting for the routine complaint investigation to finish.

### Step 3 — Investigate the Problem

Review relevant records to understand what may have happened.

Depending on the complaint, these may include:

* Raw material receiving and inspection records.
* Processing and quality-control records.
* Product grading and packaging records.
* Batch traceability information.
* Storage and delivery records.

Document the evidence collected and avoid treating an unverified assumption as a confirmed cause.

### Step 4 — Identify the Cause and Corrective Action

Distinguish between an immediate correction and a corrective action.

A **correction** addresses the detected problem, such as replacing damaged packages.

A **corrective action** addresses an identified cause to reduce the likelihood of the same problem happening again, such as revising a packaging procedure or improving equipment checks.

Record the agreed action, responsible person, and target completion date.

### Step 5 — Implement and Monitor the Action

The assigned person should update the register as the work progresses.

Management or the responsible supervisor should review overdue actions and follow up on unresolved complaints.

### Step 6 — Verify and Close the Complaint

Before closing the complaint, confirm that the required action has been completed.

Where appropriate, check whether the action was effective and document the response provided to the customer.

Record the closure date and retain relevant supporting evidence.

## 6. Using the Excel Toolkit

The proposed Excel toolkit contains five worksheets.

Each complaint should use the same Complaint ID across relevant worksheets.

Businesses should update records regularly and limit access to confidential customer and commercial information.

## 7. Reviewing Complaint Trends

Businesses can review their complaint records periodically to identify recurring issues.

Useful indicators include:

* Number of complaints received.
* Number of open and closed complaints.
* Complaints by category.
* Overdue corrective actions.
* Repeated complaints involving the same product or process.

These indicators can support management decisions, but they should be interpreted alongside production volumes, order quantities, and other relevant business information.

## 8. Important Considerations

This toolkit supports operational recordkeeping and continuous improvement. It does not replace food-safety procedures, product recall plans, legal requirements, or buyer-specific quality agreements.

Businesses should adapt the register to their operating procedures and applicable requirements.

Sensitive customer information should be handled securely and accessed only by authorized personnel.

## Conclusion

An organized complaint-handling process can help cashew businesses respond more consistently to customer concerns and identify opportunities for improvement.

By recording complaints, investigating causes, assigning corrective actions, and verifying results, businesses can strengthen accountability and improve the management of product-quality issues.

The **Mato Cashew Customer Complaint & Corrective Action Register** provides a practical starting point for building these records.

### Complaint Register — Key Fields

* **Complaint ID:** Unique reference for each complaint.
* **Date Received:** Date the business received the complaint.
* **Customer Reference:** Customer name or internal customer code.
* **Product Description:** Product name, grade, or specification.
* **Batch/Lot Number:** Identification of the affected product batch.
* **Complaint Category:** Type of issue reported.
* **Complaint Details:** Description of the problem.
* **Affected Quantity:** Quantity potentially affected.
* **Risk Assessment:** Initial evaluation of seriousness.
* **Responsible Person:** Staff member assigned to the complaint.
* **Corrective Action:** Action planned to address the identified cause.
* **Target Completion Date:** Expected completion date.
* **Status:** Open, Under Investigation, Action in Progress, or Closed.
* **Closure Date:** Date the complaint was formally closed.
