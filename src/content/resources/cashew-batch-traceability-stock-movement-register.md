Cashew processing businesses receive raw cashew nuts from different farmers, suppliers, and locations. These nuts pass through several processing stages, including drying, steaming, shelling, peeling, grading, and packaging before being sold to customers.

Without proper records, businesses may find it difficult to identify where raw materials came from, monitor stock movements, investigate quality problems, or trace products delivered to customers.

The **Mato Cashew Batch Traceability & Stock Movement Register** is a practical Excel-based toolkit designed to help cashew processors, cooperatives, and small businesses organize their records and improve inventory control.

## 1. Purpose of the Toolkit

The toolkit helps businesses:

* Record incoming raw cashew nuts and supplier information.
* Assign unique batch identification numbers.
* Connect raw materials with processing activities.
* Track finished-product quantities and grades.
* Monitor stock received, issued, transferred, and remaining.
* Record customer deliveries and dispatch information.
* Support investigations when quality or inventory problems occur.

The toolkit is intended for practical business recordkeeping and can be adapted to different processing operations.

## 2. Who Can Use This Toolkit?

This resource is suitable for:

* Small and medium-sized cashew processing enterprises.
* Agricultural cooperatives and farmer groups.
* Warehouse and inventory management staff.

## 4. How to Use the Toolkit

### Step 1 — Assign a Batch Number

Assign a unique identification number to each incoming raw cashew nut batch.

Example:

**RCN-20261116-001**

This example represents a raw cashew nut batch received on November 16, 2026.

Businesses should use a consistent batch numbering system and avoid assigning the same number to different batches.

### Step 2 — Record Supplier Information

Enter the following information when raw cashew nuts are received:

* Receiving date.
* Supplier name or reference.
* Supplier location or origin.
* Raw cashew nut batch number.
* Received quantity in kilograms.
* Available moisture or quality inspection results.
* Storage location.

Accurate receiving records help businesses identify the source of their raw materials.

### Step 3 — Connect Processing Records

When raw cashew nuts enter processing, record the processing batch number and the corresponding input batch.

Important information includes:

* Processing date.
* Raw material batch number.
* Input quantity.
* Processing batch number.
* Output quantity.
* Responsible staff member.

If several raw material batches are combined, the records should preserve the relationship between all input batches and the resulting output batch.

### Step 4 — Record Finished Products

After processing, record finished-product information such as:

* Finished-product batch number.
* Processing batch reference.
* Cashew kernel grade.
* Packed quantity.
* Packaging date.
* Storage location.

These records help connect finished products with earlier processing activities.

### Step 5 — Update Stock Movements

Record each stock transaction when products are received, issued, transferred, dispatched, or adjusted.

Businesses should regularly compare recorded stock balances with physical inventory.

Any differences should be investigated and documented.

### Step 6 — Track Customer Deliveries

When finished products are dispatched, record:

* Dispatch date.
* Customer name or reference.
* Finished-product batch number.
* Product description or grade.
* Dispatched quantity.
* Delivery document reference.

This information helps businesses identify which customers received products from a particular batch.

## 6. Recommended Recordkeeping Practices

To improve the reliability of batch traceability and stock records, businesses should:

* Use consistent and unique batch identification numbers.
* Record transactions promptly.
* Keep supporting receiving, processing, and delivery documents.
* Maintain clear links between incoming and outgoing batches.
* Review inventory balances regularly.
* Investigate unexplained stock differences.
* Protect records from unauthorized changes.
* Keep backups of important business records.
* Conduct periodic traceability exercises.

A practical traceability exercise involves selecting one finished-product batch and checking whether its raw material sources and customer deliveries can be identified.

Any missing information should be recorded and corrected.
