*News report | Event: 25 October 2026 | Source publication: 27 October 2026*

International agricultural development organisation **Agrisud International** visited **Kampong Thom province on 25 October 2026** to learn more about Cambodia’s cashew industry and explore areas where future development cooperation might be useful, according to a report published by the **Cashew Nut Association of Cambodia (CAC)** on 27 October.

The field mission was joined by **Mr. Phoeun Kimly**, Director of the Siem Reap Provincial Department of Commerce, and welcomed by **Mr. Suy Kokthean**, Vice-President of CAC. The visit was exploratory rather than the launch of a funded programme.

## Understanding the needs of Cambodia’s cashew value chain

The discussions considered the structure of the cashew value chain and practical challenges faced by farmers and other stakeholders. Areas of interest included **sustainable production**, opportunities to expand **domestic processing**, and ways to improve **marketing and market access**.

These issues are closely connected. Better farm practices may help producers manage quality and production risks, while local processing capacity can create opportunities to retain more value within Cambodia. Stronger links between growers, processors and buyers can also support more reliable commercial relationships.

## What future cooperation could mean

CAC’s account describes potential areas for collaboration, not confirmed project commitments. If a partnership is developed, technical assistance and coordinated support could potentially help smallholder families improve production practices and market participation. Investment and access to suitable finance would remain important for businesses seeking to expand processing facilities.

**No signed agreement, project budget, beneficiary count or implementation timetable was announced in the cited report.** Any future programme should therefore be reported separately once details are confirmed.

## Why this matters for Cambodia

Cambodia produces significant volumes of raw cashew nuts, while efforts to strengthen domestic processing remain a major industry priority. The Agrisud visit highlights continuing interest in practical solutions that connect farm-level productivity, value addition and market opportunities. For farmers and processors, the most important next development will be whether the initial discussions lead to a defined, funded programme.
