The National Institute of Science, Technology and Innovation (NISTI) organized a consultation and data-validation workshop in Phnom Penh on 18 September 2026 to discuss challenges affecting cashew harvesting and processing in Cambodia.

According to the Cambodia Cashew Association (CAC), the workshop brought together **46 participants**, attending in person and online. CAC was represented by **Sambath Karuna**, head of its Phnom Penh branch.

## Improving Harvesting and Processing

The discussion focused on practical ways to strengthen harvesting and primary processing, reduce post-harvest losses, improve product quality, and increase the value retained within Cambodia's cashew sector.

Participants also highlighted the importance of technical skills and better post-harvest management in supporting more efficient domestic processing.

## Practical Training for Cashew Processors

CAC reported that it provides practical cashew-processing training at its headquarters, where a demonstration processing facility supports existing processors and entrepreneurs interested in entering the sector.

Its **three-day training programme** covers post-harvest management, processing techniques, quality control, machine operation, financial management, and value addition. Participants also receive guidance on international kernel-grading standards, food safety, moisture management, equipment selection, and processing-cost calculations.

CAC President **Oun Silot** noted that funding constraints currently limit the programme to one session per year, restricting opportunities to extend technical training to more local processors.

## Why the Discussion Matters

The workshop and CAC's training efforts point to the need for continued cooperation among government institutions, industry associations, and processors. Stronger technical capacity and post-harvest practices can help reduce losses and support greater domestic value addition.
