*News report | Forum: 27–28 October 2026 | Source published: 28 October 2026*

Cambodia’s cashew processing industry needs access to suitable financing to expand local production and retain more value within the country. The **Cashew Nut Association of Cambodia (CAC)** participated in the **first National Forum on Financing for Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs)**, held on **27–28 October 2026**, to explore possible financing and market-access solutions for cashew businesses, according to CAC’s 28 October report.

## A national forum focused on financing barriers

The forum was organized by the **Agricultural and Rural Development Bank (ARDB)** in cooperation with the **Federation of Associations of Small and Medium Enterprises of Cambodia (FASMEC)**. Discussions focused on the difficulty many smaller enterprises face in securing working capital and investment funds, as well as finding reliable buyers.

Among the mechanisms discussed were **credit guarantees through the Credit Guarantee Corporation of Cambodia (CGCC)** and financing packages offered by **ARDB and SME Bank**. The forum also featured business-to-business matching to help local producers connect with potential customers.

These were **options discussed at the forum**, not an announcement that CAC members had already received financing or that a dedicated cashew lending scheme had been approved.

## Why financing matters to cashew processors

Local cashew processors need capital to purchase raw nuts during the harvest, invest in equipment, maintain inventories and manage operating expenses before sales revenue arrives. Access to appropriately structured finance could help businesses plan procurement and strengthen processing capacity.

Financing alone is not enough. Buyers, quality standards and consistent demand are also important if new processing capacity is to remain commercially viable. The forum’s market-linkage activities therefore have potential relevance alongside its lending discussions.

## What comes next?

CAC’s participation signals continued engagement with banks and other stakeholders, but the association’s report does not confirm specific loan approvals, funding amounts or agreements for individual processors. Businesses should check eligibility, costs, collateral or guarantee requirements and repayment terms directly with participating financial institutions.

For Cambodia’s cashew sector, the central question is whether discussions at the forum lead to financing that is practical for growers, traders and processors while supporting sustainable domestic value addition.
