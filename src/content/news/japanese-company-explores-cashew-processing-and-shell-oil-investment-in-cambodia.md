## Japanese Company Discusses Cashew Investment in Kampong Thom

A Japanese company is exploring opportunities to invest in cashew nut processing and cashew nut shell liquid (CNSL) production in Cambodia, as the country seeks to increase domestic processing and make better use of agricultural by-products.

According to the Cashew Nut Association of Cambodia (CAC), representatives of the association met with Mr. Keisuke Satoh, Vice President of JFE Shoji Vietnam Co., Ltd., on 12 November 2026 to discuss a potential investment in Kampong Thom province.

JFE Shoji Co., Ltd. is exploring cooperation with TPJ to develop facilities that would process raw cashew nuts and extract industrial oil from cashew shells.

## Proposed Processing Capacity and Production Plans

Under the proposed investment plan, the cashew processing facility would have an annual capacity of approximately **30,000 tonnes of raw cashew nuts (RCN)**.

The processing operation is expected to generate around **20,000 tonnes of cashew shells per year**, which could supply a separate CNSL extraction facility.

The planned CNSL plant would have an annual oil production capacity of approximately **5,000 tonnes**.

These figures represent proposed production capacities and should not be interpreted as actual output or confirmed operating facilities.

## Machinery Testing Before Investment

The project is expected to begin with testing CNSL extraction machinery at the Kampong Thom Provincial Polytechnic Institute (PIKT).

If the technical tests are successful, the partners intend to proceed with investment arrangements and construction planning in 2027.

The proposed development would begin with a cashew nut processing facility, followed by the CNSL extraction plant.

According to CAC, the facilities are expected to be located within a planned Cashew Industrial Park in Cambodia.

However, the investment has not yet been confirmed, and implementation will depend on the outcome of technical testing and subsequent business decisions.

## Turning Cashew Shells into Industrial Products

Cashew nut shell liquid is an oil extracted from cashew shells, which are generated during the processing of raw cashew nuts.

CNSL has potential industrial applications and may provide an additional source of revenue for cashew processors.

According to the Japanese technical experts cited by CAC, CNSL could be used in heavy industries and may be blended with diesel at a ratio of approximately 5–10 percent for certain industrial applications.

These applications would require appropriate technical assessment and compliance with relevant fuel and equipment standards.

The proposed project could supply CNSL to domestic industries and explore export markets, helping Cambodia create more value from cashew-processing by-products.

## Potential Benefits for Cambodia's Cashew Industry

The proposed investment could support Cambodia's cashew sector in several ways:

* Increase domestic processing of raw cashew nuts.
* Create additional value from cashew shells.
* Encourage investment in industrial processing technology.
* Support employment and technical skills development.
* Expand opportunities for local industrial supply and exports.
* Reduce the loss of economic value from underutilised processing by-products.

During the meeting, CAC Vice President Suy Kokthean welcomed the proposal and encouraged cooperation on vocational training and skills development for Cambodian workers.

The association highlighted the importance of building a skilled workforce to support the future development of the planned Cashew Industrial Park.

## Next Steps and Outlook

The proposed cooperation between JFE Shoji and TPJ represents a potential step toward a more integrated cashew processing industry in Cambodia.

By linking raw nut processing with the production of industrial materials from cashew shells, the project could help diversify revenue opportunities for local businesses.

For now, the initiative remains at the discussion and technical-testing stage. Further developments will depend on successful machinery trials, investment decisions and construction planning.

*Editorial note: All production capacities and investment timelines are proposed plans reported by CAC, not confirmed operating results.*
