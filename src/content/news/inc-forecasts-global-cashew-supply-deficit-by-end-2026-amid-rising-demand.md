The global cashew market could face a supply deficit by the end of 2026 as production declines in major growing regions while international demand continues to expand, according to the International Nut & Dried Fruit Council (INC).

In its Cashew Crop Update & Outlook published on November 18, 2026, INC reported that the Northern Hemisphere cashew crop was projected to be approximately 6% lower than in 2025, including an estimated 10% crop shortage in West Africa.

**Production and Quality Challenges**

Beyond lower production volumes, cashew quality has also deteriorated across several major origins. Raw cashew nut shipments arriving in Vietnam, India, and Cambodia from West Africa reportedly contained higher defect levels and produced lower kernel outturns compared with the previous season.

These developments have tightened the availability of high-quality raw cashew nuts, particularly premium grades.

In Vietnam, some processing operations have slowed because of limited raw material availability. Processors have also expressed concerns about sourcing from West Africa due to inconsistent quality.

**Southern Hemisphere Production Outlook**

Cashew harvests in the Southern Hemisphere were underway by September, with growing concerns about the potential effects of a strengthening El Niño.

In Tanzania, favorable weather conditions initially suggested production could remain similar to 2025 levels, although rainfall during harvesting could affect the final outcome.

Brazil, meanwhile, is expected to achieve better production in 2026 following a relatively poor 2025 harvest. However, seasonal labor shortages remain a challenge.

INC also warned that El Niño could affect crop development in 2027, although the extent of any impact remains uncertain.

**Global Demand Expected to Increase**

Despite supply constraints, global cashew consumption is expected to grow by approximately 6–7% in 2026 compared with 2025.

Demand growth is being supported by China, India, the Americas, and other Asian markets, offsetting weaker consumption in the Middle East and parts of Europe.

As a result, INC anticipates that global cashew demand could exceed available supply toward the end of 2026.

**Implications for Cambodia**

For Cambodia, developments in the international cashew market are particularly important because of the country's role as a major supplier of raw cashew nuts to regional processing markets.

Tighter global supplies could create opportunities for Cambodian producers and exporters, particularly those able to maintain consistent raw material quality.

However, possible climate-related production risks, changing international demand, and quality requirements could also affect the sector.

Cambodian farmers, processors, and exporters should therefore monitor market developments, strengthen quality control, and prepare for potential changes in sourcing and trading conditions.

The INC outlook is a market forecast rather than a confirmed year-end result. Its underlying information was prepared between mid-September and October 2026.

**Source:** International Nut & Dried Fruit Council (INC), *Cashew Crop Update & Outlook*, November 18, 2026.
