# What is Cashew?

Cashew is a tropical evergreen tree scientifically known as *Anacardium occidentale*. It is cultivated for two valuable products: the **cashew kernel**, which is widely consumed as a nutritious food, and the cashew apple, a juicy fruit used in beverages, jams, and other food products.

Native to northeastern Brazil, the cashew tree has spread to many tropical regions around the world. Today, it is grown extensively in countries such as India, Vietnam, Côte d'Ivoire, Nigeria, Tanzania, and Cambodia. Favorable climate conditions and increasing investment in the sector have made Cambodia one of the world's leading producers of raw cashew nuts.

For consumers, cashews are known as a delicious and nutrient-rich snack. For farmers and processors, they represent an important agricultural commodity with significant economic value. Understanding what a cashew is—from the tree and fruit to the edible kernel—provides a solid foundation for learning about cultivation, processing, grading, and international trade.

## Why Are Cashews Important?

Learning about cashews helps consumers make informed food choices while giving farmers, processors, exporters, and students a better understanding of one of the world's most valuable tree crops.

This guide introduces the essential facts about cashews and serves as the foundation for more advanced topics, including processing, grading standards, quality control, nutrition, and international trade.

## Scientific Classification

The cashew tree belongs to the **Anacardiaceae** family, the same botanical family as mango (*Mangifera indica*) and pistachio (*Pistacia vera*). Its scientific name is *Anacardium occidentale*, a species native to northeastern Brazil that is now cultivated throughout tropical regions worldwide.

## Origin and History

The cashew tree (*Anacardium occidentale*) is native to the tropical regions of northeastern Brazil. Indigenous communities cultivated and consumed cashews long before European explorers arrived in South America. The tree was valued not only for its edible kernel but also for its juicy cashew apple and its adaptability to warm climates.

During the sixteenth century, Portuguese traders introduced the cashew tree to parts of Africa and Asia as part of their maritime trade routes. Because the tree grows well in tropical environments with distinct wet and dry seasons, it spread rapidly across countries such as India, Mozambique, and later Southeast Asia.

Over time, cashews became an important commercial crop. Advances in cultivation, processing, and international trade transformed the cashew industry into a significant contributor to rural livelihoods and global food markets.

## The Cashew Tree

The cashew tree is a tropical evergreen species that thrives in warm climates with well-drained soil and abundant sunlight. Under favorable growing conditions, a mature tree typically reaches between 6 and 12 meters (20–40 feet) in height, although some older trees can grow even larger.

The tree develops a broad, spreading canopy that provides shade while helping protect the soil from excessive heat. Its thick branches and extensive root system make the tree relatively tolerant of drought once established, making it well suited to many tropical regions.

Cashew leaves are simple, oval-shaped, and leathery with a glossy green surface. Young leaves often appear reddish before gradually turning dark green as they mature. During the flowering season, the tree produces clusters of small, fragrant flowers that range in color from pale green to pink.

Not every flower develops into a fruit. Successful pollination results in the formation of the cashew apple, while the true fruit—the cashew nut—grows beneath it inside a hard protective shell.

## The Cashew Apple

One of the most fascinating features of the cashew tree is the **cashew apple**. Although it looks like a fruit, it is technically a swollen stem, often referred to as a *false fruit* or *accessory fruit*. The true botanical fruit is the hard shell that develops beneath the cashew apple and contains the edible cashew kernel.

The cashew apple is usually yellow, orange, or bright red when ripe. It has a sweet aroma and a juicy texture with a slightly astringent taste caused by naturally occurring tannins. Because it bruises easily and has a short shelf life, fresh cashew apples are rarely traded internationally.

Despite this limitation, the cashew apple is highly nutritious. It contains vitamin C, natural sugars, antioxidants, and dietary fiber. In many producing countries, it is processed into juices, jams, candies, syrups, vinegar, and even fermented beverages, helping farmers generate additional value from the crop.

  variant="tip"

A single cashew apple produces only **one cashew nut**. Unlike many other fruits that contain multiple seeds, each cashew apple develops a single seed enclosed within the hard shell attached to its base.

</InfoBox>

## The Cashew Kernel

The **cashew kernel** is the edible seed found inside the hard shell of the cashew fruit. It is the most valuable commercial product of the cashew tree and is widely consumed around the world as a snack, cooking ingredient, and food processing material.

Before reaching consumers, the kernel must go through several processing stages. After harvesting, the nuts are dried, roasted or steamed, carefully shelled, peeled, graded, and packed. Each step requires precision to preserve the kernel's quality, appearance, and food safety.

The shell surrounding the kernel contains **Cashew Nut Shell Liquid (CNSL)**, a naturally occurring oil that can irritate human skin. For this reason, raw cashew nuts should never be opened by hand without proper processing. Modern processing facilities use specialized equipment and trained workers to safely remove the shell while protecting the edible kernel.

High-quality kernels are evaluated according to internationally recognized grading standards. Factors such as size, color, shape, moisture content, and the presence of defects determine their commercial value in domestic and export markets.

## Nutritional Value

Cashew kernels are widely recognized as a **nutrient-dense food**, meaning they provide a high concentration of essential nutrients relative to their serving size. They contain a balanced combination of healthy fats, plant-based protein, carbohydrates, dietary fiber, vitamins, and minerals that contribute to a nutritious diet.

The majority of the fat found in cashews consists of unsaturated fats, which are generally considered more heart-friendly than saturated fats when consumed as part of a balanced diet. Cashews also provide plant protein, making them a valuable food for people seeking a variety of protein sources.

In addition to macronutrients, cashews are naturally rich in several important micronutrients, including magnesium, copper, manganese, phosphorus, zinc, and iron. These nutrients play important roles in energy metabolism, bone health, immune function, and normal cellular processes.

Like many nutrient-rich foods, cashews are best enjoyed as part of a varied and balanced diet rather than as a single source of nutrition.

## Health Benefits

Thanks to their rich nutritional profile, cashew kernels can be part of a healthy and balanced diet. While no single food can provide every nutrient the body needs, cashews offer a combination of healthy fats, plant protein, vitamins, and minerals that support normal body functions.

### Heart Health

Cashews contain predominantly unsaturated fats, which can help support heart health when they replace foods high in saturated fat as part of an overall balanced eating pattern.

### Energy Production

Minerals such as copper, magnesium, and iron contribute to normal energy metabolism. These nutrients help the body convert food into usable energy for everyday activities.

### Bone and Muscle Function

Cashews provide magnesium and phosphorus, two minerals that play important roles in maintaining healthy bones and supporting normal muscle function.

### Immune Support

Zinc and copper contribute to the normal function of the immune system. Eating a varied diet that includes nutrient-rich foods such as cashews can help meet daily nutritional requirements.

### Antioxidant Protection

Cashews naturally contain antioxidant compounds that help protect cells from oxidative stress. These compounds work together with vitamins and minerals found in many fruits, vegetables, and nuts.

## Cambodia Perspective

Cambodia has emerged as one of the world's leading producers of raw cashew nuts, thanks to its favorable tropical climate, expanding cultivation areas, and growing expertise among local farmers. Cashew farming has become an important source of income for many rural communities and plays a meaningful role in the country's agricultural economy.

Major cashew-producing provinces include **Kampong Thom, Kratie, Kampong Cham, Ratanakiri, Mondulkiri, and Preah Vihear**. These regions provide suitable soil conditions and seasonal rainfall that support healthy tree growth and high-quality nut production.

In recent years, Cambodia has placed increasing emphasis on developing its domestic cashew processing industry. Instead of exporting only raw cashew nuts, local businesses are investing in processing facilities that produce value-added products such as premium cashew kernels for international markets.

This transition creates opportunities to strengthen local industries, generate employment, improve product quality, and increase export value. As processing capacity continues to grow, Cambodia is well positioned to become not only a major producer of raw cashew nuts but also a recognized supplier of high-quality processed cashew products.

<InfoBox title="Cambodia Insight" variant="insight">

Expanding domestic processing allows more of the value created from Cambodia's cashew harvest to remain within the country, benefiting farmers, processors, businesses, and local communities.

</InfoBox>
