# Cashew Packaging Guide

Packaging is the protection system that surrounds finished cashew kernels after processing, grading, and quality control. Its role goes beyond simply holding the product: suitable packaging helps protect kernels from unwanted moisture exchange, oxygen exposure, contamination, physical damage, and handling conditions during storage and distribution.

The right packaging system depends on the product, buyer, market, packing process, distribution environment, and applicable specification. A packaging requirement should therefore not be confused with one particular packaging technology or material.

## What Is Cashew Packaging?

Cashew packaging is the combination of materials, containers, seals, handling systems, and identification used to protect kernels after processing.

A good packaging system should support the intended product condition from packing through storage, transport, distribution, and final use. Depending on the supply chain, this may involve a product-contact package, an outer carton, and transport packaging.

Packaging should be understood in two different ways:

- **Packaging requirements** describe what the package must achieve.
- **Packaging technologies** describe ways those objectives may be achieved.

<InfoBox
  type="info"
  title="Requirement Does Not Mean One Universal Technology"
>

A requirement for hermetic protection does not automatically prescribe one pouch, one material, vacuum packaging, or one gas composition. The selected system must satisfy the applicable specification, buyer, product, process, and distribution requirements.

</InfoBox>

## Why Packaging Matters

Finished kernels remain sensitive to their surrounding environment. Packaging forms the interface between the product and that environment.

Important functions include:

- Limiting unwanted moisture exchange.
- Managing exposure to air or oxygen where required.
- Protecting against dust, foreign matter, and contamination.
- Reducing physical damage during handling and transport.
- Maintaining package and seal integrity.
- Supporting product identification and traceability.
- Helping preserve the condition achieved during processing and quality control.

Packaging therefore connects directly with [cashew moisture control](/knowledge/cashew-moisture-standards/), [storage management](/knowledge/cashew-storage-guide/), and [food-safety practices](/knowledge/cashew-food-safety/).

## Understanding the Packaging System

Packaging can be viewed as a layered protection system.

### Primary Packaging

Primary packaging directly contains or protects the cashew kernels. Its performance may involve food-contact suitability, barrier properties, sealing, atmosphere management, and contamination protection.

### Secondary Packaging

Secondary packaging groups or protects primary packages. It can add mechanical protection and provide space for identification, handling information, or commercial labeling.

### Transport Packaging

Transport packaging supports stacking, handling, shipment, and distribution. It helps protect the packaged product from physical stresses through the logistics chain.

## AFI and UNECE Packaging Requirements

Two important commercial references for cashew kernels contain packaging provisions: the Association of Food Industries (AFI) specification and UNECE DDP-17.

### AFI Specifications

The **AFI Specifications for Cashew Kernels**, adopted in September 2023, apply to shipments to the United States.

AFI states that cashews shall be packed in new, clean, dry, leakproof, lead-free containers with an airtight or hermetic seal. Packaging must also have sufficient strength to protect product integrity during normal shipment and storage.

AFI includes additional provisions covering outer-container condition, gas use, carton marking, and lot traceability.

### UNECE DDP-17

**UNECE DDP-17 &mdash; Cashew Kernels (2023)** states that cashew kernels must be packed so as to protect the produce properly. Materials used inside the package must be clean and of a quality that avoids external or internal damage.

UNECE also states that cashew kernels must be packed in hermetically sealed containers and addresses suitable printing and labeling materials.

## Hermetic Packaging

A hermetically sealed package is designed to limit uncontrolled exchange between the package environment and the surrounding environment.

For cashew kernels, this concept is important because finished product needs protection from moisture exchange, contamination, and unnecessary environmental exposure during storage and shipment.

The effectiveness of a hermetic system depends heavily on **seal integrity**. A high-performance barrier material cannot provide the intended protection if the seal is incomplete, damaged, punctured, or leaking.

<InfoBox
  type="tip"
  title="Hermetic Is Not the Same as Vacuum"
>

Hermetic describes the sealed protection of the package. Vacuum describes a process in which air is reduced before sealing. A package can be hermetically sealed without being vacuum packed.

</InfoBox>

## Vacuum Packaging

Vacuum packaging reduces the amount of air inside a package before it is sealed. It can be used as part of an atmosphere-control strategy and has been used in cashew packaging.

However, vacuum should not be treated as the automatically superior choice for every product.

Suitability depends on factors such as:

- Kernel condition before packing.
- Package material and barrier performance.
- Seal quality.
- Vacuum level and packing process.
- Physical effects on the kernels.
- Storage and transport conditions.
- Shelf-life objective.
- Buyer and market requirements.

AFI also defines **blocking** as bonding of kernels in the presence of high moisture and high vacuum pressure. This illustrates why product condition and packaging conditions must be considered together.

## Gas-Flushed and Modified-Atmosphere Packaging

Gas-flushed or modified-atmosphere packaging changes the gas environment inside the sealed package. One purpose can be to manage oxygen exposure and support quality preservation.

Gas composition, however, should never be generalized across every cashew market or packaging operation.

Within its own specification, AFI states that only food-grade carbon dioxide is permitted, at a minimum mixture of **60% CO<sub>2</sub>**, with the balance nitrogen.

<InfoBox
  type="warning"
  title="AFI Gas Requirements Are Scope-Specific"
>

The AFI CO<sub>2</sub>/N<sub>2</sub> provision belongs to the AFI specification for U.S. shipments. It should not be rewritten as a global requirement that every cashew package must use the same gas mixture, nor does it mean cashews must always be nitrogen flushed.

</InfoBox>

## Packaging Materials and Barrier Properties

Rather than looking for one universally correct packaging material, processors should consider the performance required from the complete package.

Important properties include:

### Moisture Barrier

The package should provide the level of protection needed to limit unwanted moisture exchange during the intended storage and distribution period.

### Oxygen Barrier

Where oxidative quality is a concern, package design may need to control oxygen exposure as part of the overall preservation system.

### Sealability

A barrier package depends on a reliable seal. Material selection and sealing equipment must work together.

### Mechanical Strength

Packages may need resistance to puncture, tearing, compression, stacking, and other handling stresses.

### Process Compatibility

The material and container must be compatible with the selected packing process, including vacuum or gas flushing where those technologies are used.

### Food-Contact Suitability

Product-contact materials must be suitable for their intended food use and comply with applicable market and regulatory requirements.

<InfoBox
  type="info"
  title="There Is No Universal Cashew Laminate"
>

A specific film structure, plastic grade, pouch, or rigid container should not be presented as the best solution for every cashew product. Material selection depends on the required barrier performance, packing technology, distribution environment, buyer requirements, and applicable food-contact rules.

</InfoBox>

## Moisture and Oxygen Protection

Moisture and oxygen are two important environmental factors that packaging may help manage.

Finished kernel moisture is established through processing and drying. Packaging then helps protect that condition by limiting unwanted exchange with the surrounding environment. For the specification and measurement context, see the [Cashew Moisture Standards](/knowledge/cashew-moisture-standards/).

Cashew kernels also contain oils that can undergo oxidative deterioration. Package design can influence oxygen exposure, but packaging is only one part of quality preservation. Product condition, temperature, storage time, light exposure, package integrity, and other factors can also matter.

Packaging therefore supports shelf-life performance, but a packaging technology alone does not establish a guaranteed shelf life.

## Packaging Hygiene and Food Safety

Packaging should protect kernels without becoming a source of contamination.

Important controls include:

- Clean packaging materials and containers.
- Suitable product-contact materials.
- Protection from dust, pests, foreign matter, and unsanitary handling.
- Inspection for punctures, leaks, contamination, or other damage.
- Suitable printing, labeling, and adhesive materials where applicable.
- Hygienic handling during filling, sealing, and packing.

These controls complement rather than replace the broader hygiene and hazard-management practices explained in the [Cashew Food Safety](/knowledge/cashew-food-safety/) guide.

## Package Integrity During Storage and Transport

A package must remain functional after it leaves the packing line.

Common threats to integrity include:

- Weak or incomplete seals.
- Punctures and tears.
- Crushing or excessive compression.
- Poor stacking.
- Water or unsuitable environmental exposure.
- Rough handling.
- Pest damage.
- Damaged cartons or transport packaging.

Regular inspection helps identify problems before they affect more product. Storage conditions must also support the packaging system rather than work against it.

For warehouse conditions, humidity, temperature, pest control, and stock management, see the [Cashew Storage Guide](/knowledge/cashew-storage-guide/).

## Labeling and Traceability

Packaging also carries information needed to identify and trace the product.

Depending on the applicable specification, buyer, and market, information may include:

- Product or trade identification.
- Producer or packer.
- Grade.
- Net or gross weight.
- Country of origin.
- Lot, production, or date code.
- Allergen information.
- Buyer/seller-agreed marks.

AFI includes detailed carton-marking and packing-list traceability provisions within its U.S.-shipment specification.

<InfoBox
  type="info"
  title="Labeling Requirements Depend on the Market"
>

This guide explains packaging and traceability principles, not country-by-country labeling law. Exporters should verify the legal, buyer, contract, and destination-market requirements that apply to each shipment.

</InfoBox>

## How to Choose a Packaging System

A packaging decision should be based on the complete product and supply-chain context.

Consider:

1. **Product form and condition** &mdash; What is being packed and what condition must be protected?
2. **Applicable specification** &mdash; What does the buyer, contract, standard, or market require?
3. **Shelf-life objective** &mdash; What performance must the complete product/package/storage system achieve?
4. **Barrier needs** &mdash; How much moisture and oxygen protection is required?
5. **Packing technology** &mdash; Will the system use ambient sealing, vacuum, gas flushing, or another process?
6. **Storage and distribution** &mdash; What temperatures, humidity, handling, stacking, and transport stresses are expected?
7. **Package integrity** &mdash; Can the material and seal withstand the intended supply chain?
8. **Food-contact and regulatory requirements** &mdash; Are the materials suitable and compliant for the intended market?
9. **Operational practicality** &mdash; Can the system be consistently packed, inspected, handled, and traced?

The goal is not to select a technology because it sounds more advanced. The goal is to choose a packaging system that reliably meets the product's actual requirements.

## Common Packaging Mistakes

Common problems include:

- Treating hermetic and vacuum packaging as the same thing.
- Assuming cashews must always be nitrogen flushed.
- Applying one market's packaging specification globally.
- Using a high-barrier material with a weak or unreliable seal.
- Ignoring kernel condition or moisture before packing.
- Selecting materials without considering transport and handling stresses.
- Allowing punctured, leaking, wet, or damaged packages to remain in use.
- Failing to maintain clear lot identification and traceability.
- Assuming packaging alone guarantees shelf life.
- Choosing packaging based only on appearance rather than performance and compliance.
