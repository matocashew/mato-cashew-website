# Cashew Export Guide

Exporting cashews is more than moving a product across a border. A successful shipment connects a clearly defined product with buyer requirements, quality and food-safety expectations, suitable packaging, accurate documentation, applicable origin and plant-health requirements, destination-market rules, logistics, customs procedures, and traceability.

The most useful question is therefore not simply **“Which documents do I need to export cashews?”** It is:

> **“What must be verified for this product, this buyer, this origin, this destination, and this shipment?”**

That distinction is important because export requirements are not identical for every cashew product or every international market.

## What Is Cashew Export Readiness?

Cashew export readiness means that the **product, commercial agreement, quality controls, packaging, documents, regulatory checks, logistics, and traceability records** are coordinated for an intended international shipment.

A product can be technically good but still not be ready for export if, for example:

- the buyer's specification has not been confirmed;
- the package or marking does not match the transaction;
- applicable supporting documents are missing;
- destination requirements have not been checked;
- product and shipment records do not agree;
- logistics arrangements do not protect the product in transit.

Export readiness is therefore a **system**, not a single certificate or final customs step.

## Export Requirements Are Not One Universal Checklist

The actual requirements for a cashew shipment can depend on:

- Product form.
- Processing status.
- Country of origin.
- Destination country or market.
- Buyer and contract.
- Trade arrangement.
- Transport mode.
- Applicable food, customs, plant-health, and labeling rules.
- Current regulatory requirements at the time of shipment.

<InfoBox
  type="warning"
  title="Do Not Treat One Export List as Universal"
>

A document, certificate, registration, notification, or inspection required for one shipment may not apply in exactly the same way to another. Always verify the current requirements for the actual product, origin, buyer, destination, and transaction before shipment.

</InfoBox>

## Define the Product

Export preparation should start by defining exactly what is being sold.

Important information may include:

- Raw cashew nuts (RCN) or processed cashew kernels.
- Kernel grade and size.
- Product condition and specification.
- Quantity.
- Unit and bulk pack format.
- Lot or batch identity.
- Processing or formulation where relevant.

This distinction matters because requirements for raw agricultural commodities should not automatically be transferred to processed kernels.

For the commercial grading framework, see [Cashew Grading Standards](/knowledge/cashew-grading-standards/).

## Confirm Buyer and Contract Requirements

Before preparing a shipment, the seller and buyer should have a clear commercial specification.

Depending on the transaction, this may address:

- Product and grade.
- Size or count designation.
- Quality limits.
- Moisture requirement.
- Quantity and tolerance.
- Packaging configuration.
- Marking or labeling instructions.
- Testing or inspection where applicable.
- Delivery and shipment terms.
- Documentary requirements.
- Shelf-life expectations where relevant.

A buyer may require controls or evidence beyond a general industry standard or regulatory minimum. Commercial requirements should therefore be confirmed rather than inferred.

## Confirm Quality Requirements

Export quality should be tied to the product specification and the agreed acceptance criteria.

For processed cashew kernels, quality discussions may include grade, size, defects, moisture, appearance, and other characteristics relevant to the contract or applicable standard.

The complete grading and quality frameworks are covered separately in [Cashew Grading Standards](/knowledge/cashew-grading-standards/) and [Cashew Quality Standards](/knowledge/cashew-quality-standards/).

Moisture is one particularly important specification factor. For measurement and standards context, see [Cashew Moisture Standards](/knowledge/cashew-moisture-standards/).

## Confirm Food-Safety Requirements

Commercial quality and food safety are related but different.

An export-ready product should operate within an appropriate food-safety system and satisfy the requirements applicable to the product and destination. Depending on the situation, buyer or regulatory requirements may involve hygiene controls, hazard management, testing, records, facility requirements, or other evidence.

This article does not recreate those controls. See [Cashew Food Safety](/knowledge/cashew-food-safety/) for the broader food-safety framework.

<InfoBox
  type="info"
  title="Quality Approval Does Not Automatically Establish Import Compliance"
>

Meeting a buyer's grade or quality specification does not by itself prove that every food-import, plant-health, labeling, customs, or other destination requirement has been satisfied.

</InfoBox>

## Prepare Packaging and Marking

Export packaging has to protect the product while also supporting handling, identification, and traceability.

Before shipment, check as applicable:

- Package suitability for the product.
- Seal and package integrity.
- Protection during handling and transport.
- Lot or batch identification.
- Product identity.
- Grade and weight information.
- Origin information.
- Buyer-specific marks.
- Destination-market labeling or consumer information where applicable.

For packaging systems, barrier performance, seal integrity, bulk versus retail packaging, and related considerations, see the [Cashew Packaging Guide](/knowledge/cashew-packaging-guide/).

## Cambodia Export-Side Process

For a Cambodian exporter, customs export procedures form one part of shipment preparation.

At a high level, the process can be understood as:

**Prepare shipment and transaction information → Lodge the applicable customs export declaration → Provide applicable supporting documents → Customs processing / clearance → Shipment**

Cambodia's General Department of Customs and Excise administers customs procedures and export declarations. The exact declaration data, supporting documentation, procedure, and clearance requirements should be checked against the current GDCE process for the transaction.

<InfoBox
  type="warning"
  title="Use Current Cambodia Customs Requirements"
>

This guide provides a high-level export-readiness framework, not instructions for completing each customs declaration field. Verify the current procedure and required supporting documents with Cambodia's competent authorities and qualified service providers where appropriate.

</InfoBox>

## Certificate of Origin

A Certificate of Origin (C/O) provides evidence concerning the origin of goods. In Cambodia, origin certification can operate in different contexts, including **preferential** and **non-preferential** origin.

### Preferential Origin

Preferential origin is connected to an applicable trade arrangement and its rules of origin. Where a preferential claim is made, the exporter must consider the relevant agreement, origin criteria, certification mechanism, and supporting evidence.

### Non-Preferential Origin

Non-preferential origin serves an origin-certification purpose outside a preferential tariff claim. Its use and documentary requirements depend on the transaction and applicable rules.

<InfoBox
  type="warning"
  title="Certificate of Origin Is Context-Specific"
>

Do not assume that every cashew shipment requires the same Certificate of Origin or certification method. The applicable form, evidence, issuer or self-certification mechanism, and rules of origin depend on the trade arrangement, destination, transaction, and current requirements.

</InfoBox>

## Phytosanitary Requirements

Plant-health requirements need particular care because they depend on the commodity and destination.

A useful model is:

**Commodity Form + Processing Status + Origin + Destination + Current Plant-Health Rules → Applicable Requirement**

Raw cashew nuts and processed cashew kernels should therefore not automatically be treated the same.

<InfoBox
  type="warning"
  title="RCN and Processed Kernels May Have Different Plant-Health Requirements"
>

Do not assume that a phytosanitary certificate is always required—or never required—based only on the word “cashew.” Verify the actual commodity form and the current plant-health requirements of the destination before shipment.

</InfoBox>

## Destination-Market Requirements

Export clearance from the origin country does not by itself establish that a shipment satisfies every requirement in the destination market.

Destination checks may involve:

- Food-import requirements.
- Importer responsibilities.
- Plant-health controls.
- Customs entry.
- Labeling or marking.
- Contaminant or product limits.
- Notifications, registrations, certificates, or inspections where applicable.
- Product/origin-specific controls.

Two examples show why these layers must remain separate.

### United States Example

For U.S. food imports, the **FDA food-import framework** and the **USDA APHIS plant-health framework** address different regulatory areas.

Depending on applicability, FDA-related food-import considerations can include facility registration, Prior Notice, and importer-side Foreign Supplier Verification Program responsibilities. Plant-health admissibility and phytosanitary requirements are evaluated separately through USDA APHIS rules.

Processed nuts can fall under plant-product treatment different from less-processed commodities, so the exact commodity should be checked rather than assuming that all cashew forms require identical plant-health documentation.

### European Union Example

EU food-import controls also depend on the product, origin, regulatory area, and current risk-control framework.

Food of non-animal origin may be subject to general food requirements and contaminant limits, while certain product/origin combinations may be subject to increased or special controls where applicable.

## Logistics and Shipment

Once the product and compliance requirements are understood, the physical shipment must be arranged.

Important logistics considerations may include:

- Freight mode.
- Carrier or freight forwarder.
- Booking and shipment schedule.
- Transport documentation.
- Container or cargo condition.
- Package protection.
- Loading and handling.
- Transit conditions.
- Insurance where applicable.
- Customs coordination.
- Delivery instructions.

The logistics plan should protect the product rather than undermine the quality achieved during processing, packaging, and storage.

For storage and handling principles before dispatch, see the [Cashew Storage Guide](/knowledge/cashew-storage-guide/).

## Traceability and Record Consistency

Traceability becomes especially important when product information moves across several documents and organizations.

A useful consistency chain is:

**Product / Lot → Packing Information → Commercial Records → Applicable Certificates / Supporting Documents → Transport Record → Buyer / Import Record**

Names, quantities, lot references, weights, product descriptions, marks, and other relevant identifiers should be checked for consistency where they appear across the shipment record.

Good traceability helps parties identify what was shipped, connect it to production and quality records, and investigate discrepancies if they arise.

## Pre-Shipment Verification Framework

![Conceptual cashew export-readiness flow from defining the product and buyer requirements through quality, packaging, documents, destination checks, logistics, and traceable delivery.](/images/knowledge/cashew-export-readiness-flow.jpg)

*Conceptual cashew export-readiness flow. Actual documents, certificates, filings, and responsibilities depend on the product, origin, buyer, destination, shipment, and current requirements.*

Before dispatch, a processor or exporter can use the following as a **verification framework**:

1. **Product defined** — commodity form, grade, specification, quantity, and lot are clear.
2. **Buyer requirements confirmed** — commercial and technical expectations are agreed.
3. **Quality and food-safety requirements checked** — relevant controls and evidence are ready.
4. **Packaging and marking checked** — package condition, identification, and required marks are appropriate.
5. **Export-side documents prepared** — applicable commercial and customs information is ready.
6. **Origin requirements checked** — C/O or other origin evidence is prepared where applicable.
7. **Plant-health requirements checked** — phytosanitary requirements are verified for the actual commodity and destination.
8. **Destination requirements verified** — current import, food, labeling, or other applicable requirements have been checked.
9. **Logistics arranged** — carrier, freight, customs coordination, and transport documents are organized.
10. **Traceability records checked** — product, lot, packing, document, and shipment information are consistent.

<InfoBox
  type="info"
  title="A Verification Framework, Not a Legal Checklist"
>

The steps above help organize export preparation. They do not mean that the same document, certificate, filing, or authority applies to every shipment.

</InfoBox>

## Common Export Mistakes

Common mistakes include:

- Copying one generic document list and treating it as universal.
- Failing to distinguish raw cashew nuts from processed kernels.
- Assuming a phytosanitary certificate is always required for every cashew shipment.
- Assuming the same Certificate of Origin process applies to every transaction.
- Confusing exporter responsibilities with importer responsibilities.
- Meeting a buyer's grade specification but failing to verify destination requirements.
- Using outdated regulatory information.
- Preparing documents with inconsistent product, lot, quantity, or weight information.
- Ignoring package damage or seal integrity before loading.
- Treating a commercial quality standard as a complete customs or import law.
- Failing to coordinate with the buyer, importer, broker, carrier, or competent authority where their action is required.
