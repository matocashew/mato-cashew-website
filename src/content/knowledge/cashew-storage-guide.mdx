# Cashew Storage Guide

Proper storage is essential after cashew kernels have been processed, graded, and inspected. Even high-quality kernels can deteriorate when they are exposed to unsuitable temperature, excess humidity, moisture, air, pests, or poor handling conditions.

A well-managed storage system helps preserve flavor, texture, appearance, food safety, and commercial value while reducing avoidable product losses.

For processors, exporters, retailers, and consumers, understanding the main storage factors makes it easier to protect cashew quality throughout the supply chain.

## Why Proper Cashew Storage Matters

Storage is not simply the period between processing and sale. It is an active quality-control stage that helps protect the condition achieved during drying, peeling, grading, and final inspection.

Poor storage can contribute to:

- Moisture uptake.
- Loss of crisp texture.
- Mold or visible deterioration.
- Insect or pest activity.
- Off-aromas and off-flavors.
- Rancidity caused by deterioration of kernel oils.
- Reduced shelf life and commercial value.

<InfoBox
  type="info"
  title="Storage Is Part of Quality Control"
>

Good processing cannot compensate for poor storage. Once kernels are packed, their environment must continue to protect them from moisture, heat, contamination, and physical damage.

</InfoBox>

## Temperature and Relative Humidity

Cool, controlled storage helps slow quality deterioration. Temperature and relative humidity should be considered together because warmer conditions can make moisture management more difficult.

The Association of Food Industries (AFI) notes that industry experience supports proper-condition storage of cashews and cites general tree-nut guidance of approximately **0–10°C (32–50°F)** with **55–70% relative humidity**.

These figures should be understood as storage guidance rather than a universal requirement for every cashew product, package, warehouse, or destination market.

<InfoBox
  type="tip"
  title="Control the Storage Environment"
>

For commercial storage, temperature and humidity should be monitored according to product specifications, packaging system, buyer requirements, and applicable food-safety procedures.

</InfoBox>

## Moisture Control

Moisture is one of the most important factors affecting cashew storage stability. Kernels can interact with moisture in the surrounding environment, especially when packaging does not provide an effective barrier.

AFI specifications for cashew kernels set moisture at **3–5%**, measured using recognized loss-on-drying methods. This is a commercial specification and should not be treated as a universal rule for every product or market.

## Packaging and Protection

Packaging is part of the storage system. It helps separate finished kernels from the external environment and protects them during handling, warehousing, and transportation.

AFI commercial specifications call for cashews to be packed in new, clean, dry, leakproof containers with an airtight or hermetic seal and sufficient strength to protect product integrity during normal shipment and storage.

Effective packaging helps protect kernels from:

- Moisture exchange.
- Dust and foreign material.
- Insects and other pests.
- Physical damage.
- Unwanted exposure to air.
- Odor transfer from nearby materials.

<InfoBox
  type="success"
  title="Packaging Protects Stored Quality"
>

Storage conditions and packaging work together. A good warehouse cannot fully protect kernels if the package is damaged, poorly sealed, wet, or contaminated.

</InfoBox>

## Air, Heat, Light, and Rancidity

Cashews contain natural oils that contribute to their flavor and nutritional value. Over time, deterioration of these oils can produce rancid flavors or aromas.

Higher storage temperatures and unnecessary exposure to air can accelerate quality loss. Commercial storage should therefore minimize avoidable heat exposure, protect packaging integrity, and keep finished product away from direct sunlight where practical.

Rancidity should not be confused with visible mold. A kernel may develop an unacceptable off-flavor or aroma even when no visible fungal growth is present.

## Storage Hygiene and Food Safety

Storage areas are part of the food-safety system. Finished kernels should remain protected from contamination after processing is complete.

Good storage hygiene includes:

- Keeping storage areas clean and dry.
- Preventing pest access and monitoring for infestation.
- Protecting products from dust, chemicals, and foreign materials.
- Keeping packages off the floor using suitable pallets or racks.
- Separating food products from strong-smelling or contaminating materials.
- Checking packages for damage, leaks, moisture, or visible mold.
- Maintaining traceability through lot and date identification.

<InfoBox
  type="warning"
  title="Visible Mold or Infestation Requires Attention"
>

Products showing visible mold, pest activity, abnormal odors, or damaged packaging should not simply be mixed back into normal stock. They require evaluation under the facility's food-safety and quality-control procedures.

</InfoBox>

## Practical Storage Practices

A consistent storage routine helps identify problems before they affect a larger quantity of product.

### Before Storage

- Confirm that finished kernels meet the required moisture and quality specification.
- Inspect packaging for cleanliness, dryness, seal integrity, and physical damage.
- Verify lot identification and traceability information.
- Make sure the storage area is clean and ready to receive finished product.

### During Storage

- Maintain the required temperature and humidity conditions.
- Keep products protected from direct heat and sunlight.
- Store packages on suitable pallets or racks rather than directly on the floor.
- Maintain pest-control and sanitation programs.
- Avoid storing cashews near chemicals or strong-smelling products.
- Inspect stock periodically for package damage, moisture, mold, or infestation.

### Stock Management

A documented stock-rotation system helps prevent products from remaining in storage longer than intended. Commercial operators should also monitor shelf-life status and follow buyer or product specifications when releasing finished goods.

## Storage Life and Shelf Life

Storage life is not determined by time alone. Product condition at packing, moisture, temperature, relative humidity, packaging, oxygen exposure, and handling all influence how well kernels retain quality.

AFI states that empirical industry knowledge suggests it is reasonable to expect approximately **24 months of storage life when cashews are stored under proper conditions**.

This figure should not be presented as a guaranteed expiry period. A specific commercial shelf life should be established and managed using the product specification, packaging system, processing controls, storage conditions, and market requirements.

<InfoBox
  type="info"
  title="Storage Life Is Not a Guarantee"
>

A 24-month storage-life reference describes an industry expectation under proper conditions. It does not replace a product-specific shelf-life program or the date and storage instructions established by the manufacturer.

</InfoBox>

## Common Storage Mistakes

Common problems that can reduce storage performance include:

- Storing cashews in warm or humid areas without adequate control.
- Using damaged, wet, dirty, or poorly sealed packaging.
- Placing finished product directly on the floor.
- Ignoring changes in temperature or humidity.
- Mixing new stock with older stock without rotation controls.
- Storing kernels near chemicals or strong odors.
- Failing to inspect for pests, mold, package damage, or off-aromas.
- Treating a general storage-life estimate as a guaranteed expiry period.
